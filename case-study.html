AI-Powered Lead Qualification & CRM Automation

A test workflow for an air-conditioning service business that validates incoming inquiries, uses Google Gemini to score a lead, creates a traceable lead ID, assigns a technician, records the lead in Google Sheets, and routes email notifications based on the lead temperature.

n8n CloudGoogle GeminiGoogle SheetsGmailEnd-to-end test: 1 sample lead
The challenge

Problem

Manual inquiry handling can leave customer details incomplete, make lead prioritization inconsistent, and delay staff follow-up. A small service team needs a repeatable way to validate each inquiry, assess its urgency, create a reference, and keep an accessible CRM record.

The approach

Solution

I built an n8n workflow that combines rule-based validation, Gemini-generated lead assessment, structured JSON parsing, lead identification, technician assignment, Google Sheets logging, and Gmail notification routing.

The workflow is demonstrated with sample/test data. It is not represented as a production deployment or a live customer system.

Workflow architecture

The tested path runs from manual sample input through validation and AI scoring to assignment. The assigned lead then branches to CRM logging and notifications.

1Lead InputSample inquiry

4Parse AI ScoreStructured fields

6Assign TechnicianCurrent test: Matra
7CRM & RoutingSheets + Gmail branches

Implementation detail: the CRM append and Switch routing receive the lead from the assignment step as separate branches. The Switch compares lead_temperature with HOT, WARM, and COLD.

Key workflow components

01 · Data validation

Checks required fields, numeric unit count, allowed aircon types, allowed service types, and detects a matching service area in the location text.

02 · AI qualification

Gemini returns a lead score, temperature, reason, and recommended follow-up. A parser converts the returned JSON text into usable fields.

03 · Traceability

Creates a HAS-... lead reference and timestamp, and adds an assigned-technician field to the lead data.

04 · CRM record

Appends the lead information to the Lead CRM worksheet in Google Sheets so the record is available outside the workflow execution.

05 · Notifications

Sends a technician notification and uses a Switch node to select a notification branch based on the lead temperature.

06 · Operational controls

Tests use sample information and a dedicated mini-workflow so the lead-intake path can be reviewed without running the later booking/GHL stages.

Lessons learned and next improvements

Important finding: score calibration is needed. The sample message indicates the customer is only asking for pricing and is not yet sure about booking, yet Gemini returned a score of 80 and classified the lead as HOT. The workflow technically ran, but the classification may not reflect the intended business definition of “HOT.” The scoring rubric needs clearer, testable intent rules before this is treated as reliable prioritization.
  • Define explicit, deterministic scoring criteria for booking intent and validate them against HOT/WARM/COLD sample cases.
  • Test missing fields, invalid unit counts, invalid service values, and out-of-area inquiries.
  • Run separate WARM and COLD test cases and confirm exactly one matching notification branch executes.
  • Replace the fixed technician assignment with availability- or workload-based assignment when the business rules are defined.
  • Add safeguards for duplicate CRM entries and idempotent processing before live use.
  • Continue with booking confirmation, job completion, commission calculation, and GoHighLevel contact/custom-field integration as separate phases.
